Summary

Figure 1. Given 8 training views, our method maintains low Average Error across interpolated novel views, whereas the baseline exhibits overfitting. Plots are means and standard deviations over 10 runs.


3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) [1] lacks generalization across novel viewpoints in a few-shot scenario
because it overfits to the sparse observations (Fig. 1).
We reformulate the 3DGS training objective, thereby explicitly guiding 3DGS to converge toward better generalization.

Motivation

Figure 2. Conceptual 1D Loss Landscape of Flat and Sharp Minima [2].


Keskar et al. [2] show that flat minimum better generalize then sharp minimum (Fig. 2).
Motivated by this tendency, Sharpness-Aware Minimization (SAM) [3] jointly minimizes both empirical loss and sharpness,
which is equivalent to minimizing the worst-case loss within a neighborhood of radius ρ.



The model parameters are updated with the local maximum loss. In practice, the local maximum is approximated
via first-order Taylor expansion, perturbing the parameters in the gradient direction with magnitude ρ.



WSAM [4] revisits sharpness as a regularization term by weighting it. When the weight hyperparameter γ is 0, the optimization minimizes the empirical loss only. When γ = 0.5, it is equivalent to SAM, while for 0.5 < γ < 1, the sharpness term is emphasized.


Hypothesis

Figure 3. High-frequency regions requires sharp minima for accurate reconstruction,
while low-frequency regions favor flat minima.


However, directly applying SAM to 3DGS is suboptimal. The importance of sharpness for accurate reconstruction varies across pixels (Fig. 3), and since SAM regularize sharpness uniformly, it produces blurry results (Fig. 4).

Figure 4. Our method preserves fine details that SAM tends to oversmooth.


Based on this intuition, we hypothesize that for each Gaussian,
the optimal perturbation magnitude ρ and regularization weight γ vary in correlation with image frequency.
